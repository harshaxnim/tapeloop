Always listening.
Never keeping.

Tapeloop is a quiet cassette in your menu bar. It holds a rolling stretch of audio and lets the rest go, until you ask it to keep something.

In memory only.

Audio is compressed and held in memory: about 22 MB an hour per source. Nothing touches the disk until you save.

Saved after the fact.

Drag back along the timeline, or press ⌃⌥S anywhere, and the last minutes become an ordinary audio file.

Never uploaded.

Your recordings stay on your Mac. Tapeloop sends nothing anywhere and has no account, no analytics and no cloud.

Rewind, listen,
then keep.

The tape shows your voice above the line and your Mac's sound below it. Drag the handle back to the moment it started, press play to hear it without saving, and keep it with one click.

⌃⌥S Save the last minutes
⌃⌥B Bookmark this moment
⌃⌥P Pause or resume

Meetings,
caught whole.

When Zoom, Teams, FaceTime or a browser call starts using the microphone, Tapeloop marks the meeting on the tape and offers to save all of it, named after your calendar event if you allow that.

Words, on your Mac.

Optional transcripts come from NVIDIA Parakeet, running on your Mac's graphics processor. Lines are marked as you or the other side. Download the model only if you want it.

Private by design.

The buffer lives in memory and is erased when Tapeloop quits. Recordings and transcripts are plain files in a folder you choose. The only download is the optional speech model, made when you press Download and checked against its fingerprint.

The amber dot in the menu bar is always visible while recording, and macOS shows its own microphone indicator too. Recording others may need their consent where you live.
