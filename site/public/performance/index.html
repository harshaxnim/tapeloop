Performance report

Barely there.

Measured on a MacBook Air with an Apple M2, macOS 14.6, during an ordinary working session, with both sources recording. 17 November 2026, Tapeloop 0.1.0.

Tapeloop is one process. Measured over two minutes with the kernel's own accounting (the source of Activity Monitor's figures), plus leaks, vmmap and sample, with the popover closed:

The audio held comes on top of the 13 MB: about 22 MB per hour per source at the default quality, so the default two hours of both sources is about 89 MB. Almost all of the remaining CPU time is Apple's AAC encoder compressing the two sources. Meeting detection costs nothing between meetings.

Stress, soak and leak tests

A test tool drives the real recorder, encoder, buffer, disk spill and exporter with synthetic audio, far faster than real time.

TestResult
Soak: 4 hours of two-source audio into the default 2-hour buffer24 s of CPU per hour of audio (0.66 % of a core). Memory levels off. Saving 2 hours takes 0.1 s.
Soak with disk: 6 hours into a 5-hour buffer, 50 MB memory limitMemory stays at the limit, the rest goes to disk. Saving 5 hours (108 MB) takes 1.1 s.
Sponge: 21 formats, empty and huge buffers, NaN and out-of-range samples, timestamps that jump, 200 rounds of concurrent changesAll checks pass, 0 leaks. Found and fixed one bug (setting the clock back).
Speech model: load, transcribe 43 s, unload, three timesLater loads 0.02 s; 43 s of speech in 11 to 14 s; memory does not grow across cycles.

Saving copies the compressed audio without re-encoding, which is why hours save in about a second.

Compared with Rewinder

Rewinder is an open-source instant-replay app for the Mac that records the screen, system audio and microphone. Its performance report was measured on a 64 GB Mac under heavy load. Its video figures are left out, and the comparison is rough: different machines, and its capture helper handles audio and video together.

RewinderTapeloop
Processes while recording3: app, capture helper, ffmpeg1
App process memory33 MB (peak 73), capture and encoding elsewhere13.4 MB (peak 13.4) for everything, plus the audio held
Audio processing CPUffmpeg 26 to 35 % of a core (noise removal, mixing)1.1 % to capture and encode both sources
Where the buffer livesOn disk: memory flat at any lengthIn memory: no disk writes; grows with length
Disk writes while recording0.4 to 1.3 MB/s (mostly video)0
BatteryLowers the video frame rate on batteryNo special mode needed at 1 % CPU
Microphone processingNoise removal and gainNone: kept as heard
App size94 MB19 MB (DMG 9.2 MB)

Lighter in Tapeloop: one process, no disk writes, a small fraction of the CPU of a software audio chain, a smaller app. Better in Rewinder for some uses: a disk buffer keeps memory flat however long it is, and it cleans up the microphone.

Measure your own Mac

Open Tapeloop, let it record for a minute, then from a clone of the repository:

scripts/measure-performance.sh        # CPU, wake-ups, disk, memory, threads, leaks
swift build -c release --product TapeloopBench
.build/release/TapeloopBench soak 4    # the soak test
.build/release/TapeloopBench sponge --leaks

The full report, with method and caveats, is in the repository.
